The question beneath the foundation

A grain-scale mechanism.
A project-scale question.

Installing an offshore monopile changes the soil around it. In crushable sands, grain breakage can change the material response as loading progresses. Understanding that evolution is part of understanding the resistance encountered by the pile.

Our research asks when these effects matter for installation predictions, and how they can be represented in a form that supports engineering decisions. The longer-term aim is to reduce uncertainty in drivability assessments for carbonate sands.

The research approach

Particle breakage.
Represented through the loading history.

An advanced constitutive model captures breakage evolution within a large-deformation installation analysis. It connects changes in grading, soil state and effective stress with the response of the pile–soil system.

  2. 02Breakage evolvesThe grading state changes
  3. 03Soil response changesStiffness, contraction and dilation

Material behaviour

What the constitutive model represents

The formulation combines hyperelasticity with state-dependent bounding-surface plasticity, following Irani and co-authors. A grading-based breakage index, B, evolves with plastic shear work and modifies both elastic stiffness and the critical-state relation.

This changes the soil’s tendency to contract or dilate. Under limited drainage, the resulting pore-pressure response changes effective stress and can alter pile–soil contact resistance. The effect depends on the loading path; greater breakage does not imply a universal reduction in driving resistance.

B = 0 denotes the reference initial grading; B approaches 1 at the assumed ultimate grading. It is not the percentage of grains that have broken.

Key constitutive relations
B = 1 − ω / (ω + Wmax / e0)

The breakage law uses the maximum accumulated signed plastic shear work, Wmax, the initial void ratio, e0, and a calibrated work scale, ω. Retaining the maximum prevents crushing from reversing during unloading.

ψ = e − ecs(p′, B)

The state parameter, ψ, compares the current void ratio with the breakage-dependent critical-state value at mean effective stress p′. It influences plastic hardening and dilatancy. Elastic response is derived from an energy potential, with the later positive-root formulation used in this implementation.

Numerical implementation

How it is implemented in Abaqus

  1. Material update. The independently developed constitutive routine updates effective stress and internal state through a VUMAT interface. Adaptive substepping and protected rollback support the local integration.
  2. Large deformation. Abaqus/Explicit uses a Coupled Eulerian–Lagrangian (CEL) formulation. Soil moves through a fixed Eulerian mesh, while the rigid open-ended pile moves through the soil.
  3. Pore-pressure coupling. Following the coupled CEL approach of Staubach and co-authors, the temperature degree of freedom carries an excess-pore-pressure analogue. This represents generation, transport and dissipation of pressure.
  4. Contact and motion. Near-pile effective stress informs the contact treatment. Applied loading, pile inertia and changing soil reactions together determine pile motion.

MONOPILE X presents exported results from this workflow. It does not run Abaqus or a predictive machine-learning model in the browser.

Breakage-active and breakage-suppressed runs use the same constitutive framework and nominal geometry and loading. Their comparison reflects the accumulated response through the full loading sequence. Aio provides an implementation benchmark before calibration for carbonate sands.

How this could support industry

After verification and validation, resistance and soil-state histories could inform SRV/SRD assessments and equipment or installation planning. Comparison with independent records would establish whether this reduces prediction uncertainty and the associated contingency costs. CPTu interpretation and installed-soil inputs for lateral-response methods such as PISA are further development areas.

What comes next

The core implementation is operational. The next stages establish numerical consistency, calibrate carbonate-sand behaviour and test predictions against independent installation evidence. These stages define where the model can be used with confidence.

Industry applications Development pathways

From model outputs
to installation decisions.

The priority is to establish whether breakage-aware modelling improves resistance estimates and the decisions built on them. Each application below requires verification and validation for the conditions of interest.

Start with the decision.
Then define the useful output.

For developers, value lies in better-informed delivery and contingency decisions. For consultants and contractors, it lies in traceable engineering outputs that can be compared with existing assessments and measured performance.

01Drivability and installation planningPrimary translation focus

Verified histories of pile motion and toe and shaft resistance could support soil resistance to vibratory driving (SRV) and soil resistance to driving (SRD) assessments. The first study should test a defined installation question against measured response and a partner baseline.

Potential value: better evidence for equipment and installation-strategy choices, and a clearer basis for assessing refusal, delay and contingency exposure. Noise reduction would require a separate installation and acoustic assessment.

02Inputs to established drivability toolsProposed engineering workflow

GRLWEAP uses wave-equation analysis to assess pile-driving behaviour. A breakage-aware continuum analysis could inform the resistance assumptions supplied to a drivability workflow, provided the outputs are translated and checked for the relevant installation method.

This is a proposed complement to established practice. There is no live software interface, and the present vibratory comparison is not an impact-driving validation.

GRLWEAP technical overview ↗
03CPTu interpretation in crushable soilsFuture research

A future study could examine how grain crushing affects cone resistance and pore-pressure response, and what that means for deriving installation inputs. It would require paired CPTu records, laboratory characterisation and a separately verified cone-penetration analysis.

No CPTu correction or interpretation model has been developed in this demonstrator. The question is whether a breakage-aware interpretation improves agreement with independent evidence.

04Installed soil and long-term foundation responseFuture research

Installation changes the soil state around the monopile. Validated post-installation fields could provide starting conditions for subsequent foundation-response studies, including dissipation and lateral loading.

PISA-type methods use calibrated soil-reaction curves. Bringing installation effects into such assessments would require separate lateral-response analyses and calibration; a breakage index or penetration curve is not a direct PISA input.

PISA model for marine sand ↗
05Fast exploration with data-driven modelsFuture capability

A verified simulation database could support a surrogate model for exploring parameter sensitivity and prediction ranges. That would require independent-case testing, uncertainty estimates and clear limits on the conditions covered.

The current website displays exported results. It is not a live digital twin, and the eight cycle samples are not independent training cases for a predictive machine-learning model.

  1. 01 · Core implementation

    Operational

    Implement the breakage-aware constitutive model and its Abaqus CEL coupling.

    Current evidence: an operational boundary-value model and the preliminary 46-cycle comparison in Aio crushable silica sand.

  2. 02 · Numerical verification

    In progress

    Check that the constitutive implementation and coupled calculation behave as intended.

    Completion output: documented material-point benchmarks, mesh and time-step checks, and consistent contact, restart, units and force histories.

  3. 03 · Carbonate calibration

    Planned

    Calibrate the material parameters against relevant carbonate-sand laboratory data across the required stress and density range.

    Completion output: a traceable parameter set and calibration evidence for breakage, stiffness and critical-state behaviour.

  4. 04 · Independent validation

    Planned

    Compare predictions with independent laboratory or installation cases reserved from calibration, alongside an industry baseline.

    Completion output: agreed accuracy measures, prediction errors and a defined range of application.

  5. 05 · Final sensitivity analyses

    Planned

    Use the verified and validated model to assess the influence of soil parameters, pile geometry and loading conditions.

    Completion output: ranked uncertainty drivers and response ranges that industry partners can use to assess installation decisions and potential cost implications.
