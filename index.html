Model background

Model theory and implementation

Our model examines how particle crushing changes the soil response during monopile installation. It combines an advanced constitutive model with large-deformation analysis, so that changes in grading, stiffness and pore pressure can influence the resistance encountered by the pile.

  1. 01Plastic shear workLoading history accumulates
  2. 02Breakage evolvesThe grading state changes
  3. 03Soil response changesStiffness, contraction and dilation
  4. 04Pile resistance respondsPore pressure, contact and motion interact
Material behaviour

What the constitutive model represents

The formulation combines hyperelasticity with state-dependent bounding-surface plasticity, following Irani and co-authors. A grading-based breakage index, B, evolves with plastic shear work and modifies both elastic stiffness and the critical-state relation.

This changes the soil’s tendency to contract or dilate. Under limited drainage, the resulting pore-pressure response changes effective stress and can alter pile–soil contact resistance. The effect depends on the loading path; greater breakage does not imply a universal reduction in driving resistance.

B = 0 denotes the reference initial grading; B approaches 1 at the assumed ultimate grading. It is not the percentage of grains that have broken.

Key constitutive relations
B = 1 − ω / (ω + Wmax / e0)

The breakage law uses the maximum accumulated signed plastic shear work, Wmax, the initial void ratio, e0, and a calibrated work scale, ω. Retaining the maximum prevents crushing from reversing during unloading.

ψ = e − ecs(p′, B)

The state parameter, ψ, compares the current void ratio with the breakage-dependent critical-state value at mean effective stress p′. It influences plastic hardening and dilatancy. Elastic response is derived from an energy potential, with the later positive-root formulation used in this implementation.

Numerical implementation

How it is implemented in Abaqus

  1. Material update. Amin’s independently developed constitutive routine updates effective stress and internal state through a VUMAT interface. Adaptive substepping and protected rollback support the local integration.
  2. Large deformation. Abaqus/Explicit uses a Coupled Eulerian–Lagrangian (CEL) formulation. Soil moves through a fixed Eulerian mesh, while the rigid open-ended pile moves through the soil.
  3. Pore-pressure coupling. Following the coupled CEL approach of Staubach and co-authors, the temperature degree of freedom carries an excess-pore-pressure analogue. This represents generation, transport and dissipation of pressure.
  4. Contact and motion. Near-pile effective stress informs the contact treatment. Applied loading, pile inertia and changing soil reactions together determine pile motion.

MONOPILE X presents exported results from this workflow. It does not run Abaqus or a predictive machine-learning model in the browser.

Breakage-active and breakage-suppressed runs use the same constitutive framework and nominal geometry and loading. Their comparison reflects the accumulated response through the full loading sequence. Aio provides an implementation benchmark before calibration for carbonate sands.

How this could support industry

After verification and validation, resistance and soil-state histories could inform SRV/SRD assessments and equipment or installation planning. Comparison with independent records would establish whether this reduces prediction uncertainty and the associated contingency costs. CPTu interpretation and installed-soil inputs for lateral-response methods such as PISA are further development areas.

What comes next

Material-point checks and preliminary CEL results are available. Local material-update issues, restart consistency and mesh/sector sensitivity remain to be resolved or assessed. Carbonate calibration and independent installation validation are needed before making project predictions.

  1. 01 · Core implementation

    Operational

    Implement the breakage-aware constitutive model and its Abaqus CEL coupling.

    Current evidence: an operational boundary-value model and the preliminary 46-cycle comparison in Aio crushable silica sand.

  2. 02 · Numerical verification

    In progress

    Check that the constitutive implementation and coupled calculation behave as intended.

    Completion output: documented material-point benchmarks, mesh and time-step checks, and consistent contact, restart, units and force histories.

  3. 03 · Carbonate calibration

    Planned

    Calibrate the material parameters against relevant carbonate-sand laboratory data across the required stress and density range.

    Completion output: a traceable parameter set and calibration evidence for breakage, stiffness and critical-state behaviour.

  4. 04 · Independent validation

    Planned

    Compare predictions with independent laboratory or installation cases reserved from calibration, alongside an industry baseline.

    Completion output: agreed accuracy measures, prediction errors and a defined range of application.

  5. 05 · Final sensitivity analyses

    Planned

    Use the verified and validated model to assess the influence of soil parameters, pile geometry and loading conditions.

    Completion output: ranked uncertainty drivers and response ranges that industry partners can use to assess installation decisions and potential cost implications.
